From start to finish

The visit

Everything here comes from open IIIF collections, read through Mirador: the same manifests, images, annotations and search services the 2D viewer uses. Nothing is copied or converted; it streams in as you look.

1 · Enter

Arrive at the work you were looking at

Press Enter the gallery and Safari opens an immersive session. You arrive in front of the painting that was open in Mirador, at your own eye height, seated or standing. Every image in its manifest hangs around the room in order at its real size, each with a wall label from the manifest's own data.

2 · Walk

Move the way you would in a museum

  • Pinch a painting to walk up to it; pinch again to step in close. Previous and Next follow the wall.
  • Teleport anywhere: look down, pinch and hold where you want to stand, move your hand the way you want to face, and let go. Pull your hand back towards you to land turned round; lift it to cancel.
  • Turn by pinching a bare wall and flicking sideways: 45° at a time, so you never have to swivel your chair.

3 · Look closer

Lean in and the detail keeps coming

The painting streams as IIIF tiles chosen for your eyes: as you lean in, finer tiles replace coarser ones, down to the scan's own pixels. A thin bar under the frame shows while sharper detail is on its way.

  • Relief lights the brushwork as raised paint under a low, raking light.
  • Gloss lets the varnish catch the light; the glints shift as you move your head, a little differently in each eye.
  • Light: while either is on, drag the small lamp to swing the light round the work, the way a conservator rakes a lamp across a canvas.

4 · Read

Through the doorway, the books lie open

Pinch the doorway, or teleport through it, and you're in the reading room. Each book in Mirador's catalogue is open on its own lectern, its label naming the book, the pages and where it's from.

  • Pinch the right-hand page to read on and the leaf turns over; the left-hand page goes back. Books that read right to left turn the other way.
  • Paged books open as two-page spreads, starting with the cover alone; other manuscripts show one image at a time.

5 · Explore

Tools for what each work has

Under each work, a second row of buttons offers what it supports: notes where it's annotated, a lens where there are other layers, comparison for paintings. From anywhere, Search the books opens a keyboard.

Lens

Where a work has other layers (an X-ray, infrared), look through a round lens and drag it across. Only the tiles under the lens are fetched.

Compare

Pick two paintings and they leave their walls to hang side by side in front of you, both at full resolution. Done sends them back.

Search

Type on a keyboard you pinch, and every book with a IIIF Content Search service is searched. Pinch a result to walk to its book, open at the page, with the match lit.

6 · Leave

Back to Mirador, where you were

Press the Digital Crown. Mirador reopens on the work you were at, zoomed to the detail you were looking at. A book from the reading room opens in its own window, in book view, at the pages you were reading, right here on this page.

Detail and fidelity

As close as the scan goes

613 MPThe National Gallery of Art's van Gogh Self-Portrait: 21,687 × 28,273 pixels.
20 µmOf painting per pixel, at the van Gogh's true size of 57.8 × 44.5 cm.
4493 × 3604Pixels per eye on Apple Vision Pro. Tiles are chosen to match them, from where you stand.
88 fpsMeasured on Vision Pro with 146 tiles on screen.
192 MBOne shared tile budget. Tiles out of view aren't fetched.
UnlitThe art isn't tone mapped or lit by the room: its colours are shown as published.

Every work hangs at its true size, from the museum's own measurements or the dimensions in its manifest, and books lie open at their page size. Two honest limits: true size is only as good as the record, so a work whose size isn't published hangs 1 m tall and its label says so; and relief is estimated from the image's lightness, at 0.6 mm of paint per unit, not measured from the surface.

Under the hood

How it's built

A set of Mirador plugins

The gallery is a Mirador plugin: buttons in the workspace and window bars, a stage for the 3D scene, and its state in Mirador's own store. Manifests, image information, annotations and searches all go through Mirador's actions and sagas, so its request handling and IIIF auth still apply.

Open standards

IIIF Image API tiles for every image; the Presentation API for works, books, paged and right-to-left reading, and Choice layers; Web Annotations for notes; Content Search for search. WebXR in Safari for the headset.

three.js on the web

Rendered with three.js, React Three Fiber and @react-three/xr. The room is lit by an environment rendered from itself, and its oak floor is a CC0 scan. It's all a static web page: no app to install.
